You don't need to be "creative" to make things look professional. You need to know four rules, and apply them consistently.

## 1. Contrast

If two things are different, make them *obviously* different. A heading that's only slightly bigger than body text looks like a mistake, not a design choice. When in doubt, exaggerate the difference.

## 2. Repetition

Pick a handful of styles — one heading style, one button style, one spacing rhythm — and reuse them everywhere. Consistency reads as intentional, even if the design is simple.

## 3. Alignment

Every element should line up with something else on the page. Nothing should feel randomly placed. If you're not sure why something is positioned where it is, that's usually the problem.

## 4. Proximity

Related things should be close together; unrelated things should have space between them. Spacing is how people understand grouping before they've read a word.

## Why this matters more than "good taste"

Talent helps you go from good to great. These four principles take you from amateur to good — and that jump is available to anyone who applies them deliberately. Next time something looks "off" but you can't say why, run it against this list. It's almost always one of these four.
