Hierarchy is how a viewer knows what to look at first, second, and third — before reading a single word. Get it right, and a page feels obvious. Get it wrong, and everything competes for attention at once.

## Size, weight, and color all create hierarchy

You don't need all three at once. Pick the one or two that fit your context:

- **Size**: bigger draws the eye first.
- **Weight**: bold vs. regular text.
- **Color**: a single accent color used sparingly is more powerful than five competing colors.

## The most common mistake: not enough contrast

If your heading is 18px and your body text is 16px, they're barely different — the hierarchy is too subtle to register instantly. Exaggerate the difference: try 32px vs 16px, and see how much clearer the page feels.

## Exercise

Look at a page or document you made recently. Cover it and ask: what am I supposed to look at first? If you can't answer instantly, increase the contrast between your most important element and everything else.
