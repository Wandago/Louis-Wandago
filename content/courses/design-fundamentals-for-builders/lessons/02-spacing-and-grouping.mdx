Before anyone reads your content, they perceive its groupings — and spacing is what creates them.

## The proximity principle

Elements that are close together read as related. Elements with space between them read as separate. This happens instantly, before conscious reading.

A common beginner mistake is applying equal spacing everywhere. That flattens all your groupings into one undifferentiated block.

## A simple rule that fixes most spacing problems

Use **less** space between related items, and **more** space between unrelated groups. A label should sit closer to its input than to the next section. A card's title should sit closer to its own description than to the next card.

## Don't be afraid of empty space

Cramming content to "use the whole page" makes things harder to scan, not easier. Empty space isn't wasted — it's what lets the important elements breathe and stand out.

## Exercise

Pick one layout you've built. For every pair of elements, ask "are these related?" If yes, pull them closer. If no, push them apart. You'll likely need to change nothing else.
