Most non-designers either play it too safe (all gray) or go too far (five bright colors fighting for attention). There's a reliable middle ground.

## The 60-30-10 rule

A simple starting formula for any interface or document:

- **60%** neutral (background, whitespace)
- **30%** a secondary color (text, borders, secondary elements)
- **10%** one accent color, used only for what matters most (primary buttons, links, key highlights)

This keeps your accent color meaningful — because it's used sparingly, it still draws the eye when it appears.

## Pick one accent color and reuse it everywhere

Consistency matters more than the exact color you choose. If your accent is blue, every "primary action" across your entire project should be that same blue — not a slightly different blue each time.

## Don't design your own gray scale

Pure black-on-white and pure gray shades often look harsh or flat. Use a slightly warm or cool off-black and off-white instead — most design tools and frameworks (including this site's own palette) already default to this for exactly that reason.

## Exercise

Look at your last three colored elements. Could you remove any of them and still communicate the same hierarchy using just size and spacing? If yes, remove them — a smaller color palette almost always looks more intentional.
