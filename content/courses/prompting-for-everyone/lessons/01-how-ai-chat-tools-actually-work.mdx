You don't need to understand machine learning to use AI tools well. You need one correct mental model, because the wrong one causes most beginner mistakes.

## The wrong mental model: "it's a search engine"

If you think of AI chat as a search engine, you'll expect it to retrieve a single correct answer. That leads to short, search-style queries — and thin results.

## The right mental model: "it's a very well-read collaborator with no memory of you"

A better way to think about it: you're working with someone extremely well-read, fast, and willing to redo work instantly — but who has never met you before this conversation and can't see your screen, your inbox, or your intentions.

That mental model tells you exactly what to do: **give context, the way you would to a new collaborator.**

## What this means practically

- It doesn't know your audience unless you say who they are.
- It doesn't know your style unless you show it an example.
- It doesn't know what you already tried unless you mention it.
- It doesn't know when it's wrong — it will state incorrect things confidently, so you still need to verify anything factual or high-stakes.

Keep this model in mind for the rest of this course — every technique that follows is really just an application of "brief it like a new collaborator, then check its work."
