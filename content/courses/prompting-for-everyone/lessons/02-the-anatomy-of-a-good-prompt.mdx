title: "The Anatomy of a Good Prompt"
order: 2

Every prompt that reliably produces good output has some combination of these four parts.

## 1. Role or context

Who should the AI act as, or what's the situation? "You're helping me prepare for a job interview" changes the response more than any other single addition.

## 2. Task

The specific thing you want done. Be concrete: not "help with my resume" but "rewrite this bullet point to lead with the result, not the task."

## 3. Format

How you want the answer structured: a bulleted list, a table, three options ranked by effort, plain paragraphs. Without this, you'll often get more (or less) structure than you wanted.

## 4. Constraints

Length limits, tone, things to avoid, an audience to write for. Constraints narrow the space of possible answers down to the one you actually need.

## Putting it together

```
You're a career coach helping a first-time job seeker. [context]
Rewrite these three resume bullet points to lead with measurable results. [task]
Return them as a bulleted list, original next to rewritten. [format]
Keep each bullet under 20 words, no buzzwords like "team player." [constraints]
```

## Exercise

Take a task you did with AI recently that felt mediocre. Rewrite the prompt using all four parts above, and compare the outputs side by side.
